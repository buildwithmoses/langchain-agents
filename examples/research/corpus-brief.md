# Research brief

**Question:** What is driving reefer spot rates out of the Southeast this quarter, and what does it mean for Ridgeline's produce shippers?

*Prepared for Dana Reyes, VP Operations, Ridgeline Freight. Mode: corpus. Every finding below was checked in code: its quote appears verbatim in a source read during this run.*

## Answer

Southeast reefer spot rates averaged $2.87 per mile in Q3 2026, up 14%, while dry van on the same lanes rose only 4%, so the pressure is specific to reefer [1][2]. The sources point to three drivers: a produce push that ran three weeks long, Hurricane Corinne's four-day Savannah closure, and a shrinking small-reefer fleet with load-to-truck ratios at a 2021 high [3][4][5][6], with carriers citing detention and diesel at $3.94 as added cost pressure [9][11][12]. For Ridgeline's produce shippers, spring contract rates now sit below market, Ridgeline's September reefer tender acceptance fell to 78%, and the pricing desk recommends reopening Q4 rates with the top 20 produce accounts, adding a separate fuel surcharge line, and booking holiday produce 72 hours ahead [7][8]. Detention is the shipper-side lever: Ridgeline's produce detention cost rose to $41,800 in September from $23,500 in June, and only 62% was billed back to shippers [13][14].
