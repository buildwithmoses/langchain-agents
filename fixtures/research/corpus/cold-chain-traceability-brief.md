FICTIONAL DEMO DOCUMENT. This is an invented summary for a demo, loosely themed on food traceability rules. It is not the real FDA rule and is not legal advice.

The food traceability requirement summarized here applies to foods on a designated traceability list, which includes fresh tomatoes, peppers, leafy greens, melons and fresh-cut fruit.

Shippers and receivers must record key data elements for each shipment, including a traceability lot code, the quantity, and the location the food was shipped from and to. Carriers and brokers that only transport the food are not required to keep traceability lot codes, but they must be able to provide shipping documents within 24 hours when a shipper or receiver requests them during a recall.

Temperature records are not required by this rule, though many receivers now ask for continuous reefer temperature logs as a contract condition.
