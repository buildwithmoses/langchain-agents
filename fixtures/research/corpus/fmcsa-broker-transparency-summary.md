## What the rule requires

Under the broker transparency final rule summarized here, a property broker must keep an electronic record of every brokered transaction for at least three years. The record must include the shipper's payment to the broker, the broker's payment to the carrier, and any accessorial charges such as detention, layover and fuel surcharges.

When a carrier or shipper that was party to a transaction asks for the record, the broker must provide it electronically within 48 hours. Brokers may no longer include contract clauses in which carriers waive their right to see the transaction record.

## Compliance date

The rule takes effect on February 1, 2027. Brokers with fewer than 10 employees have until August 1, 2027.

## Penalties

A broker that fails to provide a record within the 48-hour window can be fined up to $10,000 per violation, and repeated violations can lead to suspension of broker operating authority.

## What brokers typically need to change

Most brokers will need to (1) remove waiver clauses from carrier agreements, (2) make sure their TMS stores shipper and carrier payment amounts on the same load record, and (3) set up a request process that can deliver records within 48 hours.
