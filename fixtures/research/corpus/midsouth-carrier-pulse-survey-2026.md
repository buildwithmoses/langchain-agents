The Fall 2026 Mid-South Carrier Pulse surveyed 412 carriers operating in Tennessee, Mississippi, Arkansas, Alabama and Georgia between August 25 and September 19, 2026. Of the respondents, 163 run refrigerated equipment.

## Capacity plans

Among reefer carriers, 22% said they plan to reduce their fleet size in the next six months, and 9% said they are considering leaving the reefer market entirely and switching to dry van. Only 11% of reefer carriers plan to add trucks.

The most common reasons given for shrinking were insurance premiums (cited by 64% of reefer carriers planning to shrink), equipment financing costs (51%), and unpaid detention time (47%).

## Detention

Reefer carriers reported an average of 3.1 hours of wait time per stop at produce facilities, compared with 1.9 hours at grocery distribution centers. Forty-one percent of reefer carriers said they now refuse loads from at least one shipper because of chronic detention.

## Broker relationships

Asked what would make them more likely to accept a broker's load, carriers ranked "detention paid within 30 days" first, "quick pay" second, and "transparent load history" third.
