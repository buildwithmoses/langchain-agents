# Southeast reefer: what moved in Q3

Reefer spot rates out of the Southeast averaged $2.87 per mile in Q3 2026, up 14% from $2.52 in Q2. Dry van rates on the same lanes rose only 4% over the same period, so the gap between reefer and dry van widened to 61 cents a mile.

Three things drove the increase. First, the late-summer Georgia and Florida produce push ran three weeks longer than usual because of a cool, wet spring that delayed planting. Second, Hurricane Corinne closed the Port of Savannah for four days in mid-September and stranded roughly 1,100 refrigerated trailers out of position. Third, reefer capacity is thinning: the Southeast lost an estimated 6% of active small reefer fleets since January, mostly owner-operators with one to five trucks.

Load-to-truck ratios on Southeast reefer lanes peaked at 11.4 to 1 in the week of September 15, the highest reading since 2021.

# What it means for our produce shippers

For produce shippers, the practical effect is that contract rates set in the spring are now well below the market. Ridgeline's contract tender acceptance on Southeast reefer fell to 78% in September, which means more of our produce shippers' loads are falling through to the spot market at higher cost.

The pricing desk recommends three actions for produce accounts: re-open Q4 contract rates with our top 20 produce shippers rather than waiting for the January bid, add a reefer fuel surcharge line separate from the linehaul rate, and book holiday-season produce at least 72 hours ahead instead of the usual 48.

We expect Southeast reefer spot rates to ease to between $2.60 and $2.70 per mile in Q4 once the produce season ends, but not to return to Q2 levels.
